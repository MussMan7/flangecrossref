How to measure for a pump flange — and which stock sizes brands actually sell

Guide · Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · ~6 min read · Not medical advice

Short answerSize the nipple diameter in millimeters (not cup size). Brands disagree on the starting math: Spectra Baby USA says measure before pumping/nursing and add about 3–5 mm; Medela’s Room to Move™ guide says measure at the base and add about 4 mm, then pick the closest shield. The 24 mm pair in the box is a default. A Spectra 24 mm and a Medela 24 mm are not guaranteed to feel the same — tunnels and rims differ. What a flange is (and isn’t)

A flange (also called a breast shield) is the hard or soft piece that seals over the breast so the pump can draw the nipple into a tunnel. Sizing is about the tunnel diameter in millimeters.

It is not bra cup size, band size, or “how big your breasts are.” Large breasts can need a small tunnel; the reverse is also true. Community threads regularly show people measuring cup size by mistake — measure the nipple.

Most double-electric kits ship with a standard pair (very often 24 mm). That is inventory convenience, not a fitted size.

How to measure

Primary manufacturer guidance agrees on the tape step even when the “add X mm” step differs:

  1. Use a ruler or measuring tape marked in millimeters.
  2. Measure the nipple diameter at the base (widest part of the nipple). Do not include the areola.
  3. Measure each side. Asymmetry is common; you may need two different tunnel sizes.
  4. Write the raw mm number down before you apply any brand’s “add X mm” rule.
Timing disagreement (do not paper over it): Spectra Baby USA recommends measuring before pumping or nursing, then adding 3–5 mm. Medela’s Room to Move guide treats measurement as a starting reference and notes nipples expand under suction (they cite average expansion figures on that page). Aeroflow’s consumer guide says many people add roughly 0–3 mm and emphasizes comfort while pumping over the tape alone. Use one brand’s rule when buying that brand’s shields, then confirm in a real session.

Why the same printed mm isn’t interchangeable

Parents regularly report that a hospital Medela 24 mm “felt smaller / better” than a Spectra 24 mm, or that a wearable InBra flange pinches at a size that works on a Spectra S1. That matches how these products are built: rim shape, tunnel length, and softness differ even when the stamp says the same number.

  • Do not assume “I am a 24” transfers from Medela → Spectra → Motif → wearable cups.
  • Do re-measure / re-test when you change brands or move from a hospital pump to a personal pump.
  • Adapters exist for some cross-brand kit mixes (for example, Pumpables publishes a Medela breastshield → Spectra backflow-protector adapter article). An adapter solves a connector problem; it does not make the tunnels identical. A dedicated adapter page is planned.

Order checklist

Screenshot before you add to cart.

  • I measured each nipple at the base in mm (areola excluded) and wrote the numbers down.
  • I know which brand + pump model the new flanges must mate with (Spectra S1/S2 kit, Medela PersonalFit Flex, wearable cups, etc.).
  • I applied that brand’s starting rule (or I’m buying a small insert variety pack to test adjacent sizes).
  • I am not assuming my old brand’s mm stamp equals the new brand’s mm stamp.
  • If crossing brands with an adapter, I also have a plan for a closed system / backflow protector where the pumps require it (see planned Medela-on-Spectra page).
  • I’m ordering duckbills / membranes on a replacement schedule separately from flanges — worn valves are a different problem than tunnel size.
  • If anything hurts, I will stop and talk to an IBCLC rather than “push through” another size order.
