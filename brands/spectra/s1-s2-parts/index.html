Spectra S1 / S2 parts: flanges, duckbills, membranes & backflow protectors

Brand parts · Updated Nov 5, 2026 (America/Denver) · ~5 min read · Not medical advice

Short answerSpectra S1 and S2 are closed-system pumps: milk stays on the flange/bottle side of a backflow protector, so tubing should not get washed. Stock plastic flanges Spectra Baby USA lists for this family are 16, 20, 24, 28, and 32 mm; kits commonly ship with 24 and 28 mm. Milk-path valves on these kits are duckbill valves (not a Medela-style separate valve membrane). The white silicone membrane lives inside the backflow protector. Replace worn duckbills and protectors on Spectra’s published cadence — flange mm and worn valves are different problems.

What belongs in an S1 / S2 milk-collection kit

Spectra Baby USA’s S1 Plus / S2 Plus instructions assemble the kit in this order (per side): duckbill valve onto the flange → backflow-protector assembly into the flange → bottle onto the flange → tubing into the backflow protector. The same instructions name these milk-contact / kit pieces for first-use sanitizing: flanges, backflow protectors, silicone valves, bottles, bottle covers, and mounting rings.

Spectra’s shop sells flanges alone, duckbill valve sets, backflow protectors, tubing, a Wide Breast Flange Set (flange + backflow protector + duckbill + tubing), and a Premium Accessory Kit (flange + bottle + backflow protector + duckbill + tubing + SG adapter end). Part names below follow those OEM product pages — not aftermarket “Spectra-compatible” listings.

Spectra’s own starting size table (instructions)

From Spectra Baby USA’s S1 and S2 Instructions (and repeated on the FAQs “Finding Your Flange Size” section). Measure nipple diameter at the base in mm; do not include the areola:

If nipple diameter is…Spectra says use…
up to 12 mm16 mm flange
up to 16 mm20 mm flange
up to 20 mm24 mm flange
up to 24 mm28 mm flange
up to 28 mm32 mm flange
Add-X wording differs on Spectra’s own site — keep both: the blog “Finding Your Flange Size” says measure before pumping/nursing and add about 3–5 mm. The Flange Sizing Guide page says add 2–3 mm (example: measure 16 mm → flange about 19–20 mm). The S1/S2 instructions give the lookup table above instead of a single add number. Use Spectra’s table when buying Spectra flanges, then confirm comfort in a real session. See also the measure & brand-sizes guide.

Silicone massager note (OEM FAQ)

Spectra’s FAQs state they also offer a Silicone Massager that inserts into flanges and converts a 24 mm or 28 mm flange into a 20 mm tunnel — compatible with those two sizes only. That is a Spectra FAQ claim, not a full insert ladder like Medela’s 15/18/21 inserts.

Duckbill valves (the milk-path valve on S1/S2)

On Spectra S1/S2 kits the valve that snaps onto the bottom of the flange is a one-piece duckbill. Spectra Baby USA’s Duckbill Valves Set product page says the set is compatible with Spectra S1, S2, 9 Plus, and SG, includes two duckbill valves, helps maintain suction, and is BPA/DEHP-free.

Duckbill vs “membrane” on Spectra: do not shop for a Medela-style separate valve membrane expecting it to be the default S1/S2 milk valve. Spectra’s white silicone membrane is the disk inside the backflow protector (next section). Spectra’s FAQs also mention a “clear valve with membrane” as an alternate attach style in assembly steps — if your kit uses that style, follow the IFU that shipped with your set. Most Spectra USA retail kits for S1/S2 use duckbills.

Cleaning: Spectra says never insert tools into duckbill valves (tears ruin the seal). Silicone duckbills are sanitized briefly (Spectra instructions: soak silicone accessories no more than about 2 minutes in boiling water; clear/hard parts longer).

Backflow protectors & the silicone membrane

The Spectra Backflow Protector product page lists compatibility with S1, S2, 9 Plus, and Synergy Gold. What’s in one protector (one breast): (1) silicone membrane, (1) upper case, (1) lower case. Double pumping needs two protectors.

Assembly (S1/S2 instructions + FAQs):

  1. Insert the white silicone membrane into the lower case.
  2. Membrane lip must go over the rim of the lower case (FAQs emphasize this for suction).
  3. Seat the upper case so all three pieces seal.
  4. Then mate the assembly to the flange and tubing.

Spectra markets the protector as a key piece of the closed system that helps keep milk out of the motor path. If suction is weak on one side only, Spectra’s FAQs suggest swapping the working side’s accessories onto the weak port — if suction follows the parts, replace the accessories.

Closed system & tubing

Spectra’s FAQs define a closed-system pump as one that does not allow air and/or bacteria to pass from the mother, down the tubing, and into the motor. On S1/S2:

  • Do not wash/sanitize tubing in normal use — Spectra’s instructions and cleaning blog say tubing should not contact milk on these pumps.
  • If tubing becomes cloudy or moldy, Spectra’s cleaning guidance is to replace it (and the FAQs warn not to run the pump with wet tubing).
  • Adapters that mix another brand’s shield onto a Spectra kit still need a working Spectra-side backflow path; that is a separate planned page (Medela shield on Spectra), not covered here.

Spectra’s Wide Breast Flange Set page separately recommends replacing parts about every 3–6 months for performance. Spectra’s suction-troubleshooting FAQ also asks whether accessories have been changed after ~3 months of ownership. Replace sooner if a duckbill no longer closes, a membrane is torn/loose, or suction collapses on one side after you rule out assembly.

Motif Luna ↔ Spectra parts claim (Aeroflow chart only)

Aeroflow’s Flange Size Charts PDF states that Motif Luna parts are compatible with Spectra S1 & S2 (and the reverse). That is a DME-chart claim. We have not verified a matching sentence on Motif’s or Spectra’s own product pages in this run — treat it as unverified for ordering until the planned Motif Luna page double-checks both makers. Do not assume every Motif wearable (Aura, etc.) shares that claim.

Manufacturer fit checks (while pumping)

Quoted from Spectra’s sizing blog and instructions — heuristics, not diagnoses:

ObservationWhat Spectra commonly points to
Nipple rubs tunnel walls; redness / sorenessMay be too small → try larger; nipple should move freely
Much of the areola pulled in; suction breaks; milk leaks at the rimMay be too large → try smaller
Nipple stretches far toward the backflow protector with pain or low outputReassess size and/or lower vacuum (Spectra notes higher vacuum is not “more milk”)
Comfortable, nipple centered with a few mm of wiggle, milk flowingWorking starting fit — re-check over time; sides can differ
Hard stop: Pumping should not hurt. Spectra’s sizing blog says if pain continues after troubleshooting size, contact an IBCLC or healthcare practitioner. This page will not claim any SKU “treats” pain, clogs, or supply.

Parts checklist before you order

Screenshot before you add to cart.

  • I know my pump is Spectra S1 / S2 (or I confirmed the part page lists my model — duckbills/BFP also list 9 Plus / SG).
  • I measured each nipple at the base in mm and matched Spectra’s table (or I’m buying adjacent OEM sizes to test).
  • I am ordering the right piece: flange mm vs duckbill pair vs full backflow protector (membrane + cases) vs tubing.
  • I am not buying a Medela valve membrane expecting it to be the default S1/S2 milk valve.
  • If suction failed on one side, I tried swapping parts across ports (Spectra FAQ) before blaming the motor.
  • I am not washing closed-system tubing “just in case.”
  • If pain continues after size/suction changes, I will stop and talk to an IBCLC — not keep ordering random mm stamps.
